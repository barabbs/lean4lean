\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

lean4lean pairs an executable re-implementation of Lean 4's trusted kernel, written in Lean 4
itself, with a Rocq-style formal development that states and partially proves the
re-implementation correct against Mario Carneiro's thesis, \emph{The Type Theory of Lean}. Two
contributed branches extend it:
\texttt{iota} (recursor reduction for inductive blocks) and \texttt{trproj} (structure
projections). This blueprint states the development as a dependency graph of roughly 950 nodes
across fourteen content chapters (Chapters~\ref{chap:syntax}--\ref{chap:experimental-reduction});
this chapter is the map.

\section{What lean4lean is}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Executable kernel} typechecks a real \texttt{Lean.Expr}/\texttt{Lean.Kernel.Environment}
environment; \texttt{Replay.lean} replays declarations from an imported environment through it.
\item \lead{Theory} an abstract model -- \texttt{VExpr}, \texttt{VEnv}, a single relation
\texttt{VEnv.IsDefEq} -- formalising the thesis's type theory.
\item \lead{Verify} a translation \texttt{TrExprS}/\texttt{TrEnv} from the executable syntax to the
model. For the parts that are finished it proves \hl{the checker accepts a term only if the model
derives its typing}: kernel soundness relative to the model, not a metamathematical consistency
result.
\item \lead{Experimental} exploratory metatheory (a logical-relations line and a
parallel-reduction line), not consumed by \texttt{Verify} and no part of the correctness argument.
\end{itemize}

\section{The thesis map}

Carneiro's thesis has eight sections; \S1 is a non-technical introduction with no counterpart
below.

\par\smallskip\noindent
\begin{tabular}{p{0.28\linewidth}p{0.65\linewidth}}
\toprule
Thesis part & Chapters and correspondence \\
\midrule
\S2 axioms & syntax (\ref{chap:syntax}), typing (\ref{chap:typing}), inductive
  (\ref{chap:inductive}, \texttt{iota}'s subject), trenv (\ref{chap:trenv}, quotients on the
  translation side), levels (\ref{chap:levels}). Levels are defined semantically, not by the
  thesis's syntactic system, and \texttt{LevelSat.lean} additionally proves the decision problem
  coNP-hard, a fact the thesis does not state; there is no $\zeta$ rule (\texttt{let} is substituted
  away); the algorithmic relation $\Leftrightarrow$ of \S2.3 is itself unformalised, its role
  played by \texttt{TypeChecker.lean} (\ref{chap:typechecker}). \\
\S3 typesys & typing (\ref{chap:typing}): Regularity, Weakening and Substitution are realised and
  generalised. Undecidability, non-transitivity and the algorithmic judgment's failure of subject
  reduction are results about the unformalised $\Leftrightarrow$; their practical stand-in is the
  \texttt{Verify/TypeChecker} correctness chain. Regularity for \texttt{iota}'s rule is the
  branch's one open obligation (\ref{thm:wf-patsstrong}). \\
\S4 unique & metatheory (\ref{chap:metatheory}): unique typing holds by a different route
  (\texttt{HasTypeStratified}, not the thesis's stratification); Church--Rosser holds with two
  \texttt{sorry}s, unrelated to either branch; definitional inversion is entirely open (three of
  its four declarations are \texttt{sorry}), untouched by both branches. Rec-normal forms and the
  $\eta$-expansion argument have no counterpart. \\
\S5 Wtypes & proj (\ref{chap:proj}), trexpr (\ref{chap:trexpr}): the eight-primitive soundness
  system is not formalised. Its $\Sigma$-projection typing shape is reused, without its $\eta$
  rule, as the model for \texttt{Expr.proj} (\ref{def:trproj}). \\
\S6 soundness & none. lean4lean proves kernel correctness relative to the \texttt{Theory} model,
  not consistency of the type theory. \\
\S7 normalization (stub) & none. \texttt{iota}'s uniform $\iota$ rule (no special case for
  subsingleton eliminators) diverges from the stub's $\kappa^+$/$\iota$ split but matches its
  weaker $\rightsquigarrow_\sigma$. \\
\S8 compilation (stub) & none. The executable kernel refuses \texttt{reduceNative} and
  \texttt{reduceBool} as unsupported. \\
\bottomrule

\section{The two contributions}
\label{sec:intro-branches}

\textbf{\texttt{iota}} gives the model an account of $\iota$-reduction, absent on master
(\texttt{VInductDecl.WF} and \texttt{VEnv.addInduct} were \texttt{sorry}, so no inductive type
entered a translated environment and no recursor computed). Chapter~\ref{chap:inductive}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Idea} $\iota$ as a schematic rewrite-rule registry (\texttt{VEnv.pats},
\ref{rule:isdefeq-pat}), one entry per recursor rule from a well-formedness specification of an
inductive block (\ref{structure:ind-wf}).
\item \lead{Result} closes master's three inductive-theory \texttt{sorry}s; \texttt{addInduct\_WF}
is a real proof, and the previously vacuous quotient-initialisation proof \texttt{addQuot.WF}
(\ref{thm:add-quot-wf}) is now proved for real, sorry-free; checked against the real kernel on 48
type formers (\texttt{Tests/IotaShape.lean}).
\item \lead{Caveat} one open \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{VEnv.WF.patsStrong} (\ref{thm:wf-patsstrong}):
it gates what was master's unconditional \texttt{Ordered.strong}, tainting unique typing and
master's primitives layer with \texttt{sorryAx}.
\item \lead{Caveat} \texttt{VInductDecl.WF} pins recursor types and $\iota$ rules as 20 syntactic
fields, where the thesis generates them from the block's specification.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{\texttt{trproj}} replaces master's opaque \texttt{TrProj := sorry}, the relation
translating Lean's \texttt{Expr.proj}, together with the seven structural lemmas that were
\texttt{sorry} as a consequence. Chapter~\ref{chap:trexpr}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Idea} a projection is an application of the recursor the kernel generates for the
eliminated structure, with a chained dependent motive for later fields (\ref{def:trproj},
\ref{struct:trprojctor}), building on the projection-expansion machinery of
Chapter~\ref{chap:proj}.
\item \lead{Result} \texttt{TrEnv.proj\_defeq} (\ref{thm:tr-env-proj-defeq}) is a fully proved,
unconditional theorem deriving the projection reduction the model needs; six of nine
projection-side \texttt{sorry}s close; checked against the kernel (\texttt{Tests/ProjShape.lean})
and against the model's own specification (\texttt{Tests/ProjInhabit.lean}).
\item \lead{Caveat} \texttt{TrProj.uniq} (\ref{thm:trproj-uniq}) is still \texttt{sorry} and gates
\texttt{TrExprS.uniq}; \texttt{TrEnv.proj\_defeq} has no consumer yet
(\texttt{reduceProjCore.WF} is still \texttt{sorry}); the general case
\ref{thm:vtc-inferproj} is not provable as stated, leaving the scoped
\ref{thm:vtc-inferproj-struct} open.
\item \lead{Caveat} built on \texttt{iota}: inherits its two open design questions.
\end{itemize}

Chapters~\ref{chap:contrib-iota} and~\ref{chap:contrib-trproj} review each branch in full;
Chapter~\ref{chap:status} gives the combined trust boundary.

\section{How to read this blueprint}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Nodes} \texttt{definition} (a \texttt{def}/\texttt{abbrev}/\texttt{structure}/
\texttt{inductive}/\texttt{instance}/\texttt{axiom}), \texttt{theorem} (an importance-3 result) or
\texttt{lemma} (a lesser or \texttt{family}-tagged one), each with a sibling \texttt{proof}, and
\texttt{example} (a test-file declaration). This chapter and
Chapters~\ref{chap:contrib-iota}--\ref{chap:status} add none.
\item \lead{\texttt{\textbackslash leanok}} on a statement means the Lean declaration exists (a
bare \texttt{sorry} still counts: the statement exists, the proof is missing). On a \texttt{proof}
it means narrower: that proof's own body has no literal \texttt{sorry}. It says nothing about
whether the lemmas it calls are fully proved.
\item \lead{\texttt{\textbackslash axfoot}} names a \texttt{sorryAx} a node inherits transitively,
through a lemma it calls rather than its own proof. Mandatory on a contributed node with such a
dependency, optional on a master node.
\item \lead{Badges} \texttt{\textbackslash contrib\{iota\textbar trproj\textbar mixed\}} marks a
node introduced or changed by a branch (\texttt{mixed}: a master declaration a branch modified).
\stProved{}, \stSorry{}, \stTainted{} and \stExperimental{} mark a node's own proof status.
\item \lead{\texttt{\textbackslash srcloc}, \texttt{\textbackslash thesisref}}
\texttt{\textbackslash srcloc\{file\}\{line\}} points at the source, pinned to the documented
commit. \texttt{\textbackslash thesisref} gives the corresponding thesis location, or states there
is none.
\item \lead{One graph} every node in the fourteen content chapters is one dependency graph, not
one per chapter. A node's border is always green (every statement carries
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok}, so the border carries no information here); its fill is the real
signal: unfilled if not proved, light green once a \texttt{definition} is stated, dark green once
a \texttt{theorem}/\texttt{lemma}'s own proof and every node it uses are sorry-free, light blue if
it could be proved but an ancestor is not. A single open \texttt{sorry} such as
\ref{thm:wf-patsstrong} or \ref{thm:trproj-uniq} caps every node depending on it at light blue.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sources and where lean4lean sits}
\label{sec:intro:sources}

Restricted to the kernel layer, the project this blueprint mirrors has two columns and two rows:
a Rocq side (MetaRocq/PCUIC) and a Lean side (Carneiro's thesis and lean4lean).

\par\smallskip\noindent
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.43\linewidth}p{0.43\linewidth}}
\toprule
 & Kernel theory & Kernel verification \\
\midrule
Rocq & PCUIC's declarative specification: universes and valuations, cumulativity, typing,
well-formed global environments ([JACM] Sec 3-4; [HAB] Ch.~2). &
MetaRocq's SafeChecker: fuel-free weak-head reduction, a universe-consistency decision
procedure, a checker proved sound \emph{and} complete ([JACM] Sec 6; [HAB] Ch.~3-5). \\
Lean & Carneiro's thesis [CT]: typing, definitional and algorithmic equality, reduction,
universes with \texttt{imax}, the inductive/recursor calculus, unique typing. &
lean4lean's executable kernel, its correctness stated relative to \texttt{VExpr}/\texttt{VEnv}/
\texttt{IsDefEq}/\texttt{HasType} (\ref{def:isdefeq}), sound but not complete. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par\smallskip

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{[CT]} Carneiro's \emph{The Type Theory of Lean}: the Lean kernel theory in full
(typing, $\equiv$, $\Leftrightarrow$, unique typing, Church-Rosser for a restricted reduction);
two chapters end unfinished, and no Lean kernel-verification result of its own.
\item \lead{[JACM]} Sozeau et al., J.~ACM 2025: PCUIC's kernel theory and a checker proved
\hl{sound and complete} against it, conjecture-free; the theorem reference of record.
\item \lead{[CCC]} Sozeau et al., POPL 2020: the earlier conference version [JACM] extends: the
first PCUIC specification and the first (sound-only) verified checker.
\item \lead{[MR]} Sozeau et al., JAR 2020: the pre-PCUIC kernel theory only; states explicitly
that its own checking algorithms have no soundness or completeness proof.
\item \lead{[HAB]} Sozeau's 2026 habilitation: a superset of the three papers above, in current
MetaRocq naming, covering the same kernel theory and verification chapters.
\item \lead{[MRD]} the MetaRocq docs site: the file-and-module map for the kernel packages, and
the tiebreaker whenever a paper and the current code disagree.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verification method.} \textbf{MetaRocq} writes its checker in Rocq against the same
syntax its specification uses, proves it sound and complete, then extracts it: the checked
object and the specified object are the same terms, with no refinement layer. \textbf{lean4lean}
ports Lean's C++ kernel onto the real \texttt{Lean.Expr}/\texttt{Lean.Kernel.Environment} and
proves soundness relative to the model through a translation \texttt{TrExprS}/\texttt{TrEnv} --
a refinement layer with its own trust boundary. It runs on explicit fuel counters rather than a
normalisation axiom, and claims soundness only: Carneiro's thesis proves Lean's definitional
equality undecidable and its algorithmic equality non-transitive, so no algorithm decides
either.

Chapter~\ref{chap:references} gives the full source list, the notion-by-notion divergence
catalogue between MetaRocq/PCUIC, the thesis and lean4lean, and the thesis-versus-code table.
