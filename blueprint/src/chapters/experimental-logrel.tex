\chapter{Experimental: logical relations}
\label{chap:experimental-logrel}

This chapter covers four modules of \texttt{Lean4Lean/Experimental/} that attack one question by
semantic means. The question is whether the abstract type theory of \texttt{SExpr} (Chapter
\ref{chap:experimental-reduction}) has \emph{definitional inversion}: sort injectivity, $\Pi$
injectivity, and the impossibility of a sort being definitionally equal to a $\Pi$. The answer is
obtained by building a finitary model, a logical relation indexed by finite approximations of terms
called \emph{shapes}, and reading the three inversion facts off the fundamental theorem of that
relation.

The files are
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1} (6100 lines, the shape algebra, the shape
interpretation, its soundness proof and the logical relation itself),
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRelAdequacy.lean}{1} (473 lines, adequacy and the three
inversion results),
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{1} (379 lines, an earlier abandoned attempt in the
\texttt{logrel-mltt} Kripke style) and
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/CoinductiveLogRel.lean}{1} (61 lines, a scratch note). All four are
byte-for-byte identical on \texttt{master} and at \texttt{trproj} HEAD, and are Mario Carneiro's
apart from two toolchain-bump commits by Kim Morrison; the \texttt{iota} and \texttt{trproj}
branches contribute \emph{zero} lines to any of them, and no node in this chapter carries an
attribution badge. The group is relevant to the review of those branches only as context: it exhibits the house style
(very large single-block tactic proofs, sparse docstrings, environments axiomatised through a
\texttt{Params} class) against which the contributions are measured.

\textbf{Thesis correspondence.} The thesis proves unique typing (\texttt{unique.tex}, Theorem
\texttt{thm:unique}) by stratifying the judgement as $\vdash_n$ by conversion-alternation depth,
showing that definitional inversion at level $n$ implies unique typing there
(\texttt{thm:utype}), and then proving definitional inversion at $n+1$ (\texttt{thm:1dinv}) through
a Church--Rosser theorem for a $\kappa$-reduction split off from proof irrelevance
(\texttt{unique.tex} \S\texttt{sec:kappa}). The \texttt{ShapeLogRel} pair is a complete alternative
to that route: no $\vdash_n$ stratification and no Church--Rosser theorem, because proof
irrelevance is absorbed into the model (a proof simply has shape $\bot$). The shape hierarchy is
the finitary, syntactic analogue of the tagged types $T_n$ and $\mathrm{tag}(t)$ of
\texttt{soundness.tex} (line 325), and \texttt{SoundTy}/\texttt{SoundEq} are literally the two
bullets of \texttt{soundness.tex} Theorem \texttt{thm:sound2} (line 351), the tagged restatement
of Theorem \texttt{thm:sound} (which has three bullets, none of them about equality of
interpretations).

\textbf{Notation.} $\Gamma \vdash M \equiv N : A$ is the \texttt{SExpr} definitional equality,
$\Gamma \vdash M : A$ its typing judgement and $\Gamma \vdash M \to^{*}_{\mathrm{wh}} N$ its
weak-head reduction (\texttt{WHRedS}). Shapes are ordered by $\le$ (``carries at least as much
information as''), joined by $\sqcup$, and $m : a$ denotes the shape-level typing judgement. All
declarations live in namespace \texttt{Lean4Lean.SExpr} and are parameterised by a
\texttt{[Params]} instance.

\textbf{Assessment.} The design idea is strong and is a genuine methodological advance over the
thesis: replacing reduction-theoretic machinery by a finite approximation model in which proof
irrelevance is absorbed rather than quarantined, so that semantic unique typing
(\ref{thm:expa-strongsound-uniq}) drops out unconditionally. Against that stand four serious
problems. The headline results are not proved: \texttt{LR.adequacy} has a live \texttt{sorry} in
its \texttt{const} case, and everything from \texttt{LE\_Interp.strongSound}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5054}) onwards additionally rests on
\texttt{SExpr.IsDefEq.strong} (a \texttt{sorry}) and on the genuine \texttt{axiom
Params.extra\_pat}, none of which is flagged anywhere in these files. The taint is sharply
delimited and the chapter should be read with that in mind: exactly 23 of the 818 declarations of
\texttt{ShapeLogRel.lean} carry \texttt{sorryAx}, and every one of them is declared at line 5054
or later, so the whole shape algebra, the shape-typing judgement, the interpretation relation and
semantic unique typing (\ref{thm:expa-strongsound-uniq}) are sorry-free. The proofs are
effectively unreviewable: seven exceed one hundred lines as single uncommented tactic blocks, and
the successor step of the relation is a 238-line structure-instance term. Documentation is thin and
badly targeted (13 docstrings in 6100 lines, none on the pivotal definitions). And
\texttt{LogRel.lean} and \texttt{CoinductiveLogRel.lean} are dead branches that are still compiled
by the \texttt{Lean4Lean.Experimental} library glob and collide, by name, with three other modules
in the same directory.

\section{The shape algebra}

\begin{definition}[The shape hierarchy]
\label{def:expa-shape}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape0,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeS,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{41}
A stratified family of finite approximations of terms and types, defined by recursion on a level
index: $\mathtt{Shape}\ 0 = \mathtt{Shape0}$ has only $\bot$ and $\mathtt{sort}\ r$, where the
boolean $r$ distinguishes $\mathrm{Prop}$ ($r = \mathtt{false}$) from $\mathrm{Type}$
($r = \mathtt{true}$); $\mathtt{Shape}\ (n{+}1) = \mathtt{ShapeS}(\mathtt{Shape}\ n)$ adds
$\mathtt{forallE}\ a\ f$ (a domain shape plus a table $f$), $\mathtt{lam}\ f$,
$\mathtt{ctor}\ c\ l$ and $\mathtt{indTy}$. Here
$\mathtt{ShapeFun}\ n = \mathtt{List}(\mathtt{Shape}\ n \times \mathtt{Shape}\ n)$ is a finite
tabulation of a monotone function on shapes.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} line 325, the sets $T_n \subseteq U_n$ of syntactically tagged
types with $\mathrm{tag}(t)$, introduced to obtain a ZFC analogue of unique typing; the level index
$n$ plays the role of the universe index.}
\reviewnote{\texttt{Shape} is an \texttt{@[implicit\_reducible] def} by recursion on
\texttt{Nat} rather than an inductive family indexed by the level. That choice is the single
largest source of the file's length: almost every lemma below is restated at level $0$, at level
$n{+}1$, and then again for \texttt{WShape} and \texttt{TShape}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Level-polymorphic constructors and smart constructors]
\label{def:expa-shape-ctors}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.sort,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.prop,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.type,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lam',Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.ctor',Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.trim,Lean4Lean.SExpr.IsStruct}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{59}
\uses{def:expa-shape}
The constructors $\bot$ and $\mathtt{sort}\ r$ are defined by recursion on the level so that they
exist at every $n$, with $\mathtt{prop} = \mathtt{sort}\ \mathtt{false}$ and
$\mathtt{type} = \mathtt{sort}\ \mathtt{true}$; $\mathtt{ShapeFun.bot} = [(\bot,\bot)]$. The smart
constructors collapse uninformative shapes: $\mathtt{lam}'\ f = \bot$ when every output of $f$ is
$\le \bot$, and $\mathtt{ctor}'\ c\ l = \bot$ when $c$ is an eta-constructor
($\mathtt{IsStruct}\ c$, i.e.\ $\mathtt{Params.classify}\ c$ is an \texttt{etaCtor}) and every field
of $l$ is $\le \bot$. $\mathtt{trim}$ applies them recursively.
\reviewnote{\texttt{Shape.trim} (\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{820}) has no
consumer anywhere in the group; it is dead code.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility of shapes]
\label{def:expa-shape-compat}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.Compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat.comm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat.symm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.Compat.def}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{72}
\uses{def:expa-shape}
A decidable, \texttt{Bool}-valued relation $s \mathbin{\mathtt{Compat}} t$ defined by structural
recursion: $\bot$ is compatible with everything, two sorts are compatible iff their flags agree,
and two shapes with the same head are compatible iff their components are. On tables,
$\mathtt{Compat}\ f\ f'$ means $\forall x \in f,\ \forall y \in f'$, compatible inputs force
compatible outputs. It is symmetric (\texttt{Compat.comm}, \texttt{Compat.symm}); that it is
equivalent to having a common upper bound is proved only later, at the well-formed level
(\ref{def:expa-wshape-join}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The information order on shapes]
\label{def:expa-shape-le}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.ble,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.ble,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.LE,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.LE,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.LE.def,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.LE.def,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.LE.trans}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{123}
\uses{def:expa-shape,def:expa-shape-compat}
A decidable preorder $s \le t$, read ``$t$ carries at least as much information as $s$'', with
$\bot$ least and matching heads compared componentwise. On tables,
$\mathtt{ble}\ f\ f'$ holds when every entry $(x,y)$ of $f$ is dominated by some entry $(x',y')$ of
$f'$ with $x' \le x$ and $y \le y'$. Comes with \texttt{LE} and \texttt{DecidableRel} instances,
reflexivity and transitivity.
\reviewnote{The order is only a preorder: two tables can dominate each other without being equal.
Consequently many downstream lemmas come in order-equivalence form, taking both $s \le t$ and
$t \le s$ (see \ref{thm:expa-hastype-mono}), which doubles their statements.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Inversion and monotonicity for the shape order]
\label{family:expa-shape-le-lemmas}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.bot_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.le_bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.le_sort,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.sort_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.le_indTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.indTy_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.forallE_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.forallE_le_forallE,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lam_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lam_le_lam,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.LE.rfl,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.LE.of_eq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.LE.rfl,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.LE.trans,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat.mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat.mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.Compat.mono_r}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{143}
A family of routine inversion lemmas characterising when a shape of each head form lies below or
above another: $s \le \bot \iff s = \bot$, $s \le \mathtt{sort}\ r \iff s = \bot \vee
s = \mathtt{sort}\ r$, $\mathtt{sort}\ r \le s \iff s = \mathtt{sort}\ r$, and analogously for
$\mathtt{indTy}$, $\mathtt{forallE}$ and $\mathtt{lam}$; together with reflexivity, transitivity
and monotonicity of compatibility in the order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\uses{def:expa-shape-le}
\leanok
Each is a \texttt{cases}/\texttt{simp} on the shape constructors at level $n{+}1$, with level $0$
handled separately because \texttt{Shape} is defined by recursion on the level.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same inversion lemmas are restated for \texttt{WShape}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1242}) and again for \texttt{TShape}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1889}). Three near-identical copies; a
\texttt{Shape}-only development with one systematic transfer lemma would have been much shorter.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Lifting a shape to another level]
\label{def:expa-shape-lift}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_self,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_le_lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.Compat.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_inj}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{284}
\uses{def:expa-shape,def:expa-shape-le}
$\mathtt{lift}\ m : \mathtt{Shape}\ n \to \mathtt{Shape}\ m$ rebuilds a shape at level $m$,
truncating to $\bot$ what does not fit (everything but $\bot$ and sorts when $m = 0$). For
$n \le m$ it is a full embedding: $\mathtt{lift}_n = \mathrm{id}$,
$\mathtt{lift}_{m} \circ \mathtt{lift}_{k} = \mathtt{lift}_{m}$, it reflects and preserves $\le$,
is injective, and preserves compatibility. The proofs are inductions on the level difference and on
the shape.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} line 325 ff., the $(\mathtt{ulift}, m, A) \in T_n$ clause with
$\mathrm{tag}((\mathtt{ulift},m,A)) = \mathrm{tag}(A)$: lifting a tagged type to a higher universe
without changing its meaning.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Partial lowering of shapes]
\label{def:expa-shape-plift}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.plift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.plift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.olift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.olift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.plift_eq_lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.plift_thm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.le_plift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.plift_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.olift_thm}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{421}
\uses{def:expa-shape-lift}
A partial inverse of \texttt{lift}:
$\mathtt{plift} : \mathtt{Shape}\ n \to \mathtt{Shape}\ m \times \mathtt{Option}(\mathtt{Shape}\ m)$
returns the best $m$-level approximation from below together with, when one exists, an exact
$m$-level preimage; $\mathtt{olift}$ is the \texttt{Option} component alone. The characterising
property is a Galois connection, for $n \le m$. In one direction (\texttt{le\_plift}),
$t \le (\mathtt{plift}\ s).1 \iff \mathtt{lift}_m\ t \le s$; dually (\texttt{plift\_le}),
$(\exists z,\ (\mathtt{plift}\ s).2 = \mathtt{some}\ z \wedge z \le t) \iff s \le \mathtt{lift}_m\ t$.
Proved by induction on the shape through the \texttt{Option} monad.
\reviewnote{Because those proofs \texttt{simp} through the elaborated \texttt{do} notation, the
whole file pins \texttt{set\_option backward.do.legacy true} at
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{10}, referencing leanprover/lean4\#13305 and
digama0/lean4lean\#31. That is a file-wide back-compatibility switch for a localised problem.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Join of shapes]
\label{def:expa-shape-join}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.mem_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.lift_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.bot_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.join_bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.sort_join_sort}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{745}
\uses{def:expa-shape-le,def:expa-shape-compat}
A total binary operation computed structurally: $\bot$ is a unit, two sorts join to themselves when
their flags agree and to $\bot$ otherwise, matching heads are joined componentwise, and mismatched
heads give $\bot$. Two tables are joined by collecting, for every pair of entries with compatible
inputs, the entry $(j, f(j) \sqcup f'(j))$ where $j$ is the join of the two inputs
(\texttt{ShapeFun.mem\_join} characterises membership in the result). It commutes with
\texttt{lift}. It is a least upper bound only on compatible arguments; that fact is proved at the
well-formed level (\ref{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Well-formed shapes]
\label{def:expa-shape-wf}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.WF,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.WF,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.NonZero,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.ListNonZero,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.WF.lift_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.WF.olift}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{829}
\uses{def:expa-shape,def:expa-shape-join}
A table is well formed when it (a) contains an entry with input $\bot$, (b) is closed under joins
of compatible inputs up to order-equivalence, (c) is monotone ($x_1 \le y_1$ implies
$x_2 \le y_2$), and (d) has well-formed components. A shape is well formed when its tables are,
with two extra side conditions: a $\mathtt{lam}\ f$ requires $f$ to be \texttt{NonZero} (some
output not $\le \bot$), and a $\mathtt{ctor}\ c\ l$ requires $l$ to be \texttt{ListNonZero}
whenever $\mathtt{IsStruct}\ c$. Well-formedness is preserved and reflected by \texttt{lift}.
\reviewnote{These \texttt{NonZero} side conditions are exactly what makes $\mathtt{lam}'$ and
$\mathtt{ctor}'$ collapse to $\bot$, and they are what encodes proof irrelevance at the shape level.
This is the key design idea of the whole development and it is documented nowhere in the file.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Well-formed shapes as a subtype, with a custom eliminator]
\label{def:expa-wshape}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.sort,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.indTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.forallE,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.lam,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.lam',Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.ctor,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.ctor',Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.elems,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.casesOn,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.casesOn'}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{928}
\uses{def:expa-shape-wf}
$\mathtt{WShape}\ n$ is the subtype of well-formed $\mathtt{Shape}\ n$ and $\mathtt{WShapeFun}\ n$
the subtype of well-formed tables, equipped with a \texttt{Membership} instance, extensionality,
the lifted constructors (with $\mathtt{lam}$ taking the \texttt{NonZero} proof and $\mathtt{lam}'$
falling back to $\bot$), and the hand-written case analysers $\mathtt{casesOn}'$ (at level $n{+}1$)
and $\mathtt{casesOn}$ (at any level), which present a well-formed shape as one of
$\bot$/sort/forallE/lam/ctor/indTy with the side conditions already unpacked.
\reviewnote{The two docstrings on these eliminators are among only 13 docstrings in 6100 lines. The
\texttt{WShape} layer then duplicates essentially every \texttt{Shape} lemma
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1055}--1400): pure boilerplate caused by defining
\texttt{Shape} by \texttt{Nat}-recursion plus a separate \texttt{WF} predicate instead of one
well-formed-by-construction inductive family.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Application of a shape function to a shape]
\label{def:expa-shape-app}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.maxBelow,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.trunc,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.app_mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.app_mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.app_mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.app_mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.app_mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.Compat.app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.lam_app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.lam'_app}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{717}
\uses{def:expa-shape-join,def:expa-shape-le,def:expa-wshape}
$f\ @\ x$ first truncates the table to the entries whose input is $\le x$ (\texttt{trunc}) and then
takes the output of the entry whose input dominates all the others (\texttt{maxBelow}, defaulting
to $\bot$). For a well-formed table that maximum exists, because the table is closed under joins of
compatible inputs. The operation is extended to $\mathtt{Shape}$ (returning $\bot$ unless the head
is a $\mathtt{lam}$), to $\mathtt{WShape}$ and to $\mathtt{TShape}$, and is shown monotone in both
arguments, compatible with lifting, and compatible with joins.
\reviewnote{The definition is scattered: \texttt{ShapeFun.app} at line 721, the well-formedness
lemma \texttt{ShapeFun.WF.app} at \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2270},
\texttt{Shape.app} at 2292 and \texttt{WShape.app} at 2304.}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compatible well-formed shapes have a least upper bound]
\label{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.exists_max,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.wf_trunc,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.trunc_compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.app_core,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.of_compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.join_mem',Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.compat_app_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.compat_app_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join_prop.ih_fun}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1557}
\uses{def:expa-shape-join,def:expa-wshape}
For $x, y : \mathtt{WShape}\ n$: (i) if $x$ and $y$ have any common upper bound then they are
compatible, and (ii) if they are compatible then $x.1 \sqcup y.1$ is well formed and is a least
upper bound, i.e.\ $x.1 \sqcup y.1 \le z \iff x \le z \wedge y \le z$ for every well-formed $z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{def:expa-shape-wf,def:expa-shape-app}
\leanok
Induction on the level $n$. The successor case is factored into the \texttt{WShape.join\_prop}
namespace (\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1391}), which takes the inductive
hypothesis as a section \texttt{variable} and proves the function-table case (\texttt{ih\_fun}) via
a maximum-element argument (\texttt{exists\_max}) and truncation (\texttt{wf\_trunc},
\texttt{trunc\_compat}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The \texttt{variable (ih : ...)} plus \texttt{include ih} idiom used to thread the induction
hypothesis through a 160-line namespace makes the helper lemmas (\texttt{exists\_max},
\texttt{app\_core}, \texttt{of\_compat}) look like standalone results with undocumented
hypotheses. \texttt{WShape.join\_prop.join\_mem'} also shadows the earlier
\texttt{WShapeFun.join\_mem'} (\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1374}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Join on well-formed shapes and the Join relation]
\label{def:expa-wshape-join}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.Join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.Compat.iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.Join.mk,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.Join.iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.Join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.ofElems}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1627}
\uses{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop}
$x \sqcup y$ on well-formed shapes is the underlying join when $x$ and $y$ are compatible and $\bot$
otherwise, so that the result is again well formed. The relational form
$\mathtt{Join}\ x\ y\ z$ says $\forall w,\ z \le w \iff x \le w \wedge y \le w$;
\texttt{Join.mk} produces it from compatibility, \texttt{Join.iff} translates between the two
presentations, and \texttt{Compat.iff} characterises compatibility as existence of a common upper
bound.
\reviewnote{Both the function \texttt{join} and the relation \texttt{Join} are carried everywhere
with \texttt{Join.iff} to translate, doubling the API surface for little apparent benefit.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Level-erased shapes]
\label{def:expa-tshape}

$\mathtt{TShape} = \Sigma n,\ \mathtt{WShape}\ n$ forgets the level. Order, compatibility and join
are defined by lifting both arguments to the maximum of their two levels;
\texttt{TShape.LE.def} says that any common upper bound of the two levels may be used instead. The
decomposition lemmas turn $\le$ between two $\mathtt{forallE}$ (resp.\ $\mathtt{lam}'$) shapes into
componentwise inequalities after lifting.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} line 325 ff.: \texttt{TShape} is the analogue of
$\bigcup_n T_n$, and its order mirrors the $(\mathtt{ulift},m,A)$ clause identifying a tagged type
with its lift.}
\reviewnote{This is a third full copy of the order/join/compatibility API.
\texttt{TShape.type} exists (\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{1887}) but
\texttt{TShape.prop} does not, an asymmetry with both \texttt{Shape} and \texttt{WShape}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Singleton shape functions]
\label{def:expa-shape-single}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.single,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.single,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ShapeFun.WF.single,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.single_app,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.single_le,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.compat_single,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShapeFun.Join.app_l}

\end{definition}

\section{The shape-level typing judgement}

\begin{definition}[Shape typing]
\label{def:expa-shape-hastype}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.hasType.core,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.hasType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasDom,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasTypePi,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasTypeLam,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasDom,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypePi,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypeLam,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasTypeLam}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2482}
\uses{def:expa-shape-app,def:expa-shape-le,def:expa-wshape}
A decidable \texttt{Bool}-valued relation $m : a$, ``the element-shape $m$ inhabits the type-shape
$a$'', by simultaneous recursion on both arguments. The clauses are: $\bot : a$ whenever $a$ is
itself a type ($a = \bot$, a sort, $\mathtt{indTy}$, or a $\mathtt{forallE}$ whose table is a
family of types over its domain); $\mathtt{sort}\ r : \mathtt{sort}\ j$ iff $j = \mathtt{true}$,
i.e.\ only $\mathrm{Type}$ classifies sorts; $\mathtt{forallE}\ a\ b : \mathtt{sort}\ r$ when $b$
has domain $a$ and all its outputs are $r$-sorts; $\mathtt{lam}\ f : \mathtt{forallE}\ a\ b$ when
$b$ has domain $a$ with every output a type, $f$ has domain $a$, and each output of $f$ inhabits
the corresponding $b\ @\ x$; $\mathtt{ctor} : \mathtt{indTy}$; and
$\mathtt{indTy} : \mathrm{Type}$. The auxiliary
\texttt{HasDom}, \texttt{HasTypePi} and \texttt{HasTypeLam} name the table-level side conditions;
everything is lifted to \texttt{WShape} and, through lifting to a common level, to \texttt{TShape}.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} line 325 ff., the clause deriving $(\Pi,A,B) \in T_n$ from
$A \in T_n$ and $B : \mathrm{tag}(A) \to T_n$: shape typing is the syntactic analogue of membership
in a tagged type.}
\reviewnote{The boolean definition exists only to obtain decidability; all reasoning goes through
the inductive characterisation \ref{thm:expa-hastype-unfold}. The recursion is structural in the
implicit level index (the \texttt{hasType} handed to \texttt{hasType.core} is the level-$n$ one),
which nothing in the source says.}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Inductive characterisation of shape typing]
\label{thm:expa-hastype-unfold}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasTypeU,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasType.unfold,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Shape.HasType.unfold_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypeU,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.unfold,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.unfold_iff}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2513}
\uses{def:expa-shape-hastype}
The boolean relation is equivalent to an inductively presented judgement \texttt{HasTypeU} with one
rule per head form: $\bot : x$ from $x : \mathrm{Type}$; $\mathtt{sort}\ r : \mathrm{Type}$;
$\mathtt{forallE}\ a\ b : \mathtt{sort}\ r$ from \texttt{HasTypePi};
$\mathtt{lam}\ f : \mathtt{forallE}\ a\ b$ from \texttt{HasTypeLam};
$\mathtt{ctor}\ c\ l : \mathtt{indTy}$; and $\mathtt{indTy} : \mathrm{Type}$. This is the inversion
principle used everywhere below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Forwards by \texttt{split} on the defining match of \texttt{Shape.hasType} followed by
\texttt{constructor} and \texttt{simp [hasType.core.iff]}; backwards by case analysis on the
derivation, with the $\bot$ case recursing into the inversion of its premise.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity of shape typing]
\label{thm:expa-hastype-mono}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasDom.mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasDom.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypeLam.mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypePi.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypeLam.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.mono_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.find_cycle,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.mono_l.ih_dom,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.mono_l.ih_pi,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.mono_l.ih_lam}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2681}
\uses{thm:expa-hastype-unfold,def:expa-shape-le}
Two monotonicity principles. \texttt{mono\_r}: if $m : a$, $a \le a'$ and $a' : \mathtt{sort}\ r$
then $m : a'$. \texttt{mono\_l}: if $m : a$, $m \le m'$ \emph{and} $m' \le m$ then $m' : a$; the
hypothesis is order-equivalence, not just $\le$. Both are stated again for \texttt{TShape}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{def:expa-shape-app}
\leanok
\texttt{mono\_r} is an induction on the level using \texttt{HasTypeU} inversion, with the
$\mathtt{lam}$ case factored through the local recursors \texttt{ih\_dom} and \texttt{ih\_lam}.
\texttt{mono\_l} lives in its own namespace with the inductive hypothesis as a section variable and
needs a pigeonhole lemma \texttt{find\_cycle}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2716}): in a finite list carrying a transitive
relation in which every element points to some element, every element reaches a reflexive point.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{find\_cycle} is a genuinely reusable combinatorial lemma buried as a \texttt{private
theorem} in the middle of a shape file; it belongs in \texttt{Lean4Lean/Std} or in Batteries. That
\texttt{mono\_l} needs order-equivalence rather than $\le$ is a real limitation which propagates to
every use site: the \texttt{mono\_l} field of \ref{structure:expa-logrel} has to carry two
\texttt{HasType} premises to compensate.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Shape typing is closed under joins]
\label{thm:expa-hastype-join}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasDom.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypePi.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasTypeLam.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.join',Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.join'}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{2960}
\uses{thm:expa-hastype-unfold,def:expa-wshape-join}
If $m_1 : a$ and $m_2 : a$ and $m_1$ is compatible with $m_2$ then $m_1 \sqcup m_2 : a$; the primed
variants take the join relationally, and the type-shape may be joined as well. This is what makes
the set of shapes approximating a term a directed set with suprema.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop,def:expa-shape-app}
\leanok
A 75-line induction on the level with case analysis on the head forms, using
\ref{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop}, \texttt{HasTypeU} inversion and the table-membership lemmas
\texttt{WShapeFun.join\_mem} and \texttt{ShapeFun.mem\_join}; the domain and lambda cases are
factored into local recursors \texttt{go\_dom} and \texttt{go\_lam}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Shape-level proof irrelevance and retyping]
\label{thm:expa-hastype-proofirrel}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.proofIrrel,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.proofIrrel,Lean4Lean.SExpr.WShape.HasType.retype,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.retype,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.LE.le_forall,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Forall,Lean4Lean.SExpr.TShape.HasType.ty_forallE_inv}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3211}
\uses{thm:expa-hastype-unfold,def:expa-shape-wf}
\texttt{proofIrrel}: if the type-shape $a$ satisfies $a : \mathrm{Prop}$ (that is,
$a : \mathtt{sort}\ \mathtt{false}$) and $x : a$, then $x = \bot$. \texttt{retype}: if
$a : \mathtt{sort}\ r$, $a' : \mathtt{sort}\ r'$ and $a \le a'$ then $a : \mathtt{sort}\ r'$.
\texttt{TShape.LE.le\_forall} inverts $\le$ against a $\mathtt{forallE}$ shape through the
auxiliary inductive \texttt{LE\_Forall}, and \texttt{ty\_forallE\_inv} says that anything
inhabiting a $\mathtt{forallE}$ shape is $\bot$ or a $\mathtt{lam}'$.
\thesisref{\texttt{axioms.tex}, the proof-irrelevance rule of definitional equality, and
\texttt{unique.tex} \S\texttt{sec:kappa}, where proof irrelevance is exactly what breaks
Church--Rosser. Here it is absorbed into the model, which is why this development avoids the
$\kappa$ / $\equiv_p$ split entirely.}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
By \texttt{HasTypeU} inversion on both premises: at level $0$ nothing but $\bot$ inhabits a
$\mathrm{Prop}$-shape; at level $n{+}1$ the only remaining case is a $\mathtt{lam}\ f$ inhabiting a
$\mathtt{forallE}$ whose codomain table is a family of $\mathrm{Prop}$s, and the inductive
hypothesis forces every output of $f$ to be $\bot$, so the smart constructor $\mathtt{lam}'$
collapses it to $\bot$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the conceptual heart of the approach: the model has no room for the content of a proof,
because every inhabitant of a proposition-shape is $\bot$. It has no docstring.
\end{remark}

\section{The shape interpretation}

\begin{definition}[Shape valuations for de Bruijn contexts]
\label{def:expa-valuation}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.nil,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.push,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.LE,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.Compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.join}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3282}
\uses{def:expa-tshape}
$\mathtt{Valuation} = \mathbb{N} \to \mathtt{TShape}$ assigns a level-erased shape to every de
Bruijn index. \texttt{nil} sends everything to $\bot$, \texttt{push} extends at index $0$, and
order, compatibility and join are pointwise, with \texttt{LE.push} splitting the order on an
extended valuation.
\reviewnote{Two of the file's thirteen docstrings sit on \texttt{Valuation.Compat} and
\texttt{Valuation.join}, the two most obvious definitions in the file, while \texttt{LE\_Interp}
immediately below has none.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The shape interpretation relation]
\label{def:expa-le-interp}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.RHS,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Const}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3333}
\uses{def:expa-valuation,def:expa-shape-hastype,def:expa-shape-app,class:expb-sexpr-params,inductive:pattern-core}
$\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m\ M$ says that the shape $m$ is a lower bound for the meaning of the
term $M$ under the valuation $\rho$. Its rules are: $\bot$ interprets anything; a variable
$\mathtt{bvar}\ i$ takes any $m \le \rho\ i$; $\mathtt{sort}\ \ell$ takes
$m \le \mathtt{sort}(\ell \neq 0)$; an application $F\ A$ takes $m \le f\ @\ a$ from shapes $f$ of
$F$ and $a$ of $A$; a lambda takes $m \le \mathtt{lam}'\ f$ when the domain $A$ has shape $a$, $f$
has domain $a$, and for every $x : a$ the body is interpreted by $f\ @\ x$ under $\rho.\mathtt{push}\
x$; a $\Pi$ takes $m \le \mathtt{forallE}\ b\ f$ under the same conditions with two domain shapes
$b$ and $b'$ for $B$, the table condition using $b'$; and a constant takes whatever the auxiliary
\texttt{Const}, \texttt{Matches} and \texttt{RHS} judgements derive from its declared type and from
the environment's $\delta$ and $\iota$ pattern rules.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} \S Modeling Lean in ZFC (line 118) and \S Type injectivity (line
325): the relation plays the role of $m \le [\![\Gamma \vdash M]\!]_\gamma$ in
the tagged model, but as a finitary, one-sided syntactic relation rather than a ZFC-valued
denotation.}
\reviewnote{The single most important definition of the file has no documentation whatsoever, and
its \texttt{const} rule is unusually subtle: it quantifies over an abstract relation $R$ that is
assumed closed under \texttt{LE\_Interp}, a parametricity trick used to handle recursive $\delta$
unfolding. Nothing in the source explains it.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Smart constructors and inversions for the interpretation]
\label{family:expa-le-interp-intro}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.bvar',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.bvar0,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.sort',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.app',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.lam',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.forallE',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.bvar_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.le_sort,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.le_sort'}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3352}
Specialisations of the rules with the trailing $\le$ premise instantiated to reflexivity, together
with the inversions $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m\ (\mathtt{bvar}\ i) \iff m \le \rho\ i$ and, for a
sort, $m \le \mathtt{sort}(\ell \neq 0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\uses{def:expa-le-interp}
\leanok
The smart constructors are immediate applications of the rules. The inversions are one-step case
analyses, using that the $\bot$ rule always yields a shape below $\bot$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity of the shape interpretation]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-mono}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.unlift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.RHS.mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Const.mono,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.RHS.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.mono_l,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Const.mono_l}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3378}
\uses{def:expa-le-interp,def:expa-tshape}
The relation is downward closed in the shape ($m' \le m$ and $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m\ M$ give
$\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m'\ M$) and monotone in the valuation ($\rho \le \rho'$ preserves

auxiliaries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
Induction on the derivation. Every rule ends in a $\le$ premise, so shape monotonicity is a
transitivity step at each constructor; valuation monotonicity only has to push through

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Shape-level pattern matching is deterministic]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-matches}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.varN_const_head,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.arity,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.head_wf,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.head_wf_eq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.matches_inter,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.compat_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.unique,Lean4Lean.SExpr.pat_arity,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Matches.lift}

expected arity (\texttt{varN\_const\_head}, \texttt{arity}), two matches of the same pattern against
the same head agree (\texttt{unique}), two matches of \emph{different} patterns against
componentwise compatible argument shapes force the two patterns to intersect
(\texttt{matches\_inter}), and two matches of the \emph{same} pattern against componentwise
compatible arguments are themselves compatible and can be joined (\texttt{compat\_join}).
\thesisref{\texttt{axioms.tex} \S the $\iota$ computation rule and \S definitions

\end{lemma}

\leanok

\texttt{Params.pat\_uniq} is not used in this block; its first use is at
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3931}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}

\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[The shapes of a term form a directed set]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-compat-join}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.compat_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.compat,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.join',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Const.compat_join,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.Const.compat_mismatch,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.RHS.compat_join}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{3936}
\uses{def:expa-le-interp,def:expa-wshape-join}
If $\rho' \le \rho$, $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho'\ m_1\ M$ and $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m_2\ M$,
then $m_1$ and $m_2$ are compatible and $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ (m_1 \sqcup m_2)\ M$. Any two
shapes approximating the same term can therefore be combined, which is what lets arbitrary finite
approximations be assembled into a witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{thm:expa-le-interp-matches,thm:expa-wshape-join-prop,thm:expa-hastype-join,thm:expa-le-interp-mono}
\leanok
Not one mutual induction but four separate ones stacked up: \texttt{RHS.compat\_join} (3561),
\texttt{Const.compat\_mismatch} (3751--3785), \texttt{Const.compat\_join} (3786--3934), and
finally a 139-line induction on the \texttt{LE\_Interp} derivation itself (3936--4074). They lean
on \texttt{Matches.compat\_join}, \ref{thm:expa-wshape-join-prop} and
\ref{thm:expa-hastype-join}; the hardest case is a $\delta$ or pattern mismatch between two
unfoldings of the same constant (\texttt{Const.compat\_mismatch}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{Const.compat\_join} alone runs from line 3786 to line 3934, 149 lines with no intermediate
structure and no comments. Only \texttt{LE\_Interp.strongSound} (206 lines), the \texttt{LRS} term
(238) and \texttt{LR.adequacy} (327) are longer.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Substitution and weakening for the shape interpretation]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-subst}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.subst,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.inst,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.weak,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.weak_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.weak'_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.closed,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.closed_iff}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{4087}
\uses{def:expa-le-interp}
$\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ m\ (M[\sigma])$ holds if and only if there is a valuation $\rho'$ with
$\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho'\ m\ M$ and $\mathtt{LE\_Interp}\ \rho\ (\rho'\ i)\ (\sigma\ i)$ for
every index $i$. Specialised to single instantiation (\texttt{inst}), weakening (\texttt{weak}) and
closed terms (\texttt{closed}).
\thesisref{\texttt{typesys.tex} \S properties of substitution (\texttt{thm:subst}); this is the
semantic counterpart of the substitution lemma.}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{thm:expa-le-interp-mono,thm:expa-le-interp-compat-join}
\leanok
The forward direction generalises over an arbitrary term whose substitution equals the given one,
inducts on the derivation, and builds $\rho'$ by a conditional that pins the shape at the
substituted index; the binder cases join the valuations produced by the sub-derivations using
\ref{thm:expa-le-interp-compat-join}. The reverse direction is a direct induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{LE\_Interp.subst} is an exact biconditional with an existentially quantified valuation and
is one of the best-designed results in the file.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Inversion of the interpretation at Pi and lambda shapes]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-inv}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.forallE_inv,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.forallE_inv',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.lam_inv,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LE_Interp.lam_inv'}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{4241}
\uses{def:expa-le-interp,def:expa-tshape}
If $\forall B.\,F$ is interpreted by $\mathtt{forallE}\ b\ f$ then $b$ interprets $B$ and, for every

\begin{definition}[Fitting valuations and typed interpretation]
\label{def:expa-fits-interptyped}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Valuation.Fits,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped.bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped.mk,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped.out,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped.hsort,Lean4Lean.SExpr.InterpTyped.hsort'}

\section{Soundness of the interpretation}

\begin{theorem}[Typed-interpretation cases for application, lambda and Pi]
\label{thm:expa-le-interp-sound-cases}

\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{4361}

The inductive steps of soundness, each stated as a hypothetical. Given that every shape of $F$ is
typed at $\forall A.\,B$ and that every shape of $B[X]$ can be raised to a type shape, every shape
of $F\ X$ is typed at $B[X]$ (\texttt{sound\_app}); analogously \texttt{sound\_lam} types a lambda

$\mathrm{Sort}(\mathrm{imax}\ u\ v)$. \texttt{sound\_bot} is the trivial $\bot$ case.

\end{theorem}

\uses{thm:expa-hastype-join,thm:expa-le-interp-inv,def:expa-shape-single}
\leanok

and assembles a witness by joining table entries. \texttt{sound\_lam} and \texttt{sound\_forallE}
each induct over the finite table of the lambda or $\Pi$ shape, using singleton tables
(\ref{def:expa-shape-single}) to build a well-formed \texttt{HasTypeLam} or \texttt{HasTypePi}
step by step.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\texttt{sound\_lam} is about 105 lines and \texttt{sound\_forallE} about 100, both single tactic
blocks with no comments.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Semantic soundness predicates and strong soundness]
\label{def:expa-soundeq-strongsound}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.SoundEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.SoundTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.StrongSound,Lean4Lean.SExpr.StrongSoundCore,Lean4Lean.SExpr.StrongSoundEq}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{4607}
\uses{def:expa-fits-interptyped,def:expa-le-interp}
$\mathtt{SoundEq}\ \Gamma\ M\ N$ says that under every fitting valuation $M$ and $N$ have exactly
the same shape interpretations; $\mathtt{SoundTy}\ \Gamma\ M\ A$ says that under every fitting
valuation every shape of $M$ is typed at a shape of $A$. \texttt{StrongSound} and
\texttt{StrongSoundCore} are a mutually inductive strengthening of the typing judgement:
\texttt{StrongSound} bundles $\Gamma \vdash M : A$, \texttt{SoundTy}, and a syntax-directed core
derivation at some type $A'$ with $\mathtt{SoundEq}\ \Gamma\ A'\ A$. \texttt{StrongSoundEq} bundles
a definitional equality with \texttt{SoundEq} and strong soundness of both sides.
\thesisref{\texttt{soundness.tex} Theorems \texttt{thm:sound} (line 216) and \texttt{thm:sound2}
(line 351): the two bullets of the soundness theorem are literally \texttt{SoundTy} and
\texttt{SoundEq}.}
\reviewnote{\texttt{StrongSoundCore.const} takes a function producing a \texttt{CtorBundle} for
\emph{every} classification of the constant, which looks over-general, since a constant has one
classification. It is worth checking whether that hypothesis is ever instantiated non-trivially.}
\end{definition}

\section{The shape-indexed logical relation}

\begin{definition}[The \texttt{LogRel} interface]
\label{structure:expa-logrel}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LogRelBase,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LogRel,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LogRelBase.TyDefEq.sort}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5265}
\uses{def:expa-wshape,def:expa-shape-hastype,family:expb-sexpr-operational}
$\mathtt{LogRelBase}\ \Gamma\ n$ carries two predicates over $\mathtt{WShape}\ n$:
$\mathtt{DefEq}\ M\ N\ A\ m\ a$ (``$M$ and $N$ are equal at type $A$, element-shape $m$ and
type-shape $a$'') and $\mathtt{TyDefEq}\ A\ B\ a$ (``$A$ and $B$ are equal types at shape $a$'').
$\mathtt{LogRel}$ extends it with nineteen closure obligations: at a sort shape both relations say
that the two sides weak-head reduce to the \emph{same} sort (\texttt{sort\_iff},
\texttt{sort\_iff\_ty}); everything is related at element-shape $\bot$ provided the type-shape is a
type, $a : \mathrm{Type}$ (\texttt{bot}); \texttt{toType} turns a \texttt{DefEq} at a sort
type-shape into a \texttt{TyDefEq} at the \emph{element} shape; then left-reflexivity
($M \equiv N$ gives $M \equiv M$: \texttt{left}, \texttt{left\_ty}), symmetry (\texttt{symm},
\texttt{symm\_ty}), three transitivities (\texttt{trans}, \texttt{trans'},
\texttt{trans\_ty}), conversion (\texttt{conv}), four monotonicity fields (\texttt{mono\_r\_2},
\texttt{mono\_r\_2\_ty}, \texttt{mono\_r\_1}, \texttt{mono\_l}), closure of \texttt{TyDefEq}
under joins of compatible shapes (\texttt{join\_ty}), and invariance under weak-head reduction of
both sides (\texttt{whr}, \texttt{whr\_ty}).
\reviewnote{Bundling the obligations as a record is good design: it lets the successor step
\ref{def:expa-lrs} be written once, generically in its predecessor. The two field docstrings (lines
5266 and 5268) are the only documentation here; the nineteen obligations carry none, and several are
not obvious, in particular \texttt{trans'} (a heterogeneous transitivity at sort types) and
\texttt{mono\_r\_1}, which needs an extra premise $\mathtt{TyDefEq}\ A\ A\ a'$.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The relation at shape level zero]
\label{def:expa-lr0}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR0,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR0.TyDefEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR0.DefEq}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5298}
\uses{structure:expa-logrel,family:expb-sexpr-operational}
At level $0$ a well-formed shape is $\bot$ or a sort, so the relation collapses to a statement about
weak-head normal forms. $\mathtt{LR0.TyDefEq}\ \Gamma\ M\ N$ is $\mathrm{True}$ at $\bot$, and at
a sort shape it says that some sort $\mathrm{Sort}\ u$ is a common weak-head reduct of $M$ and of
$N$. $\mathtt{LR0.DefEq}$ matches on the type-shape, giving $\mathrm{True}$ at $\bot$ and
$\mathtt{LR0.TyDefEq}$ at a sort. All nineteen obligations are discharged by
\texttt{simp}/\texttt{split}/\texttt{trivial}, the three transitivity fields using determinacy of
weak-head reduction (\texttt{WHRedS.determ}).
\thesisref{\texttt{unique.tex} Theorem \texttt{thm:0dinv} (line 56), ``$\vdash_0$ has definitional
inversion'': the base case, reindexed by shape level instead of by conversion-alternation depth.}
\reviewnote{Demanding the \emph{same} $u$ on both sides is exactly where sort injectivity enters the
model, and the rest of the development exists to propagate this one clause upwards. Nothing in the
source says so.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Pi-edge and lambda validity at level $n+1$]
\label{def:expa-lrs-components}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.PiDefEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.ValTyPi2,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.LamDefEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.PiDefEq.left,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.LamDefEq.left}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5376}
\uses{structure:expa-logrel,def:expa-shape-app}
Three predicates relative to a predecessor relation $\mathit{IH} : \mathtt{LogRel}\ \Gamma\ n$.
$\mathtt{PiDefEq}\ \mathit{IH}\ B\ F_1\ F_2\ b\ f$ is a conjunction: for all arguments $x, y$ with
$\Gamma \vdash x \equiv y : B$ related at a shape $p : b$, the instantiated codomains $F_i[x]$ and
$F_i[y]$ are equal types at $f\ @\ p$ for $i = 1,2$; and for every single well-typed argument $x$ at
$p : b$, $F_1[x]$ and $F_2[x]$ are equal types at $f\ @\ p$. Next,
$\mathtt{ValTyPi2}\ \mathit{IH}\ M_1\ M_2\ b\ f$ says that
$M_1$ and $M_2$ weak-head reduce to $\Pi$s whose domains are convertible and equal types at $b$ and
whose codomains are convertible (under the first domain) and satisfy $\mathtt{PiDefEq}$. $\mathtt{LamDefEq}$ is the same pattern
one level down for terms: applying $M$ and $N$ to related arguments gives results related at
$(m\ @\ p,\ a_2\ @\ p)$. The \texttt{left} lemmas project a two-sided instance to a reflexive one.
\reviewnote{The docstrings that start appearing here are refactoring notes (``merged
\texttt{PiEdgeDefEq} / \texttt{PiEdgeEq2}'', line 5373) rather than specifications. They are not
the file's first: \texttt{WShape.casesOn'} (1001), \texttt{WShape.casesOn} (1020),
\texttt{Valuation.Compat} (3297), \texttt{Valuation.join} (3301) and the two \texttt{LogRelBase}
fields (5266, 5268) precede them, six of the file's thirteen.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Type equality at level $n+1$]

\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.left,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.symm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.trans,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.bot,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.sort_iff,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.lam',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.ctor',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.TyDefEq.forallE_iff}

\begin{theorem}[The relation is invariant under shape lifting]
\label{thm:expa-lr-lift}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR.DefEq.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR.TyDefEq.lift,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LR.lift_succ_aux,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.PiDefEq.lift_aux,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRS.LamDefEq.lift_aux}

between the lifted shapes is equivalent to the relation at level $n$ between the originals:
$\mathtt{LR}.\mathtt{DefEq}\ M\ N\ A\ (\mathtt{lift}_{n'} m)\ (\mathtt{lift}_{n'} a)$ holds
if and only if $\mathtt{LR}.\mathtt{DefEq}\ M\ N\ A\ m\ a$ does, and likewise for
$\mathtt{TyDefEq}$ when
$m : \mathrm{Type}$. This is what makes the level index irrelevant and lets $\mathtt{TShape}$-indexed
statements be used against a level-indexed relation.
\end{theorem}

\uses{thm:expa-hastype-mono,def:expa-lrs-defeq}
\leanok

about $\mathtt{DefEq}$ and $\mathtt{TyDefEq}$ proved by case analysis on the shape; the $\Pi$ and
lambda cases are delegated to the private auxiliaries \texttt{LRS.PiDefEq.lift\_aux} and
\texttt{LRS.LamDefEq.lift\_aux}, which take both induction hypotheses as explicit arguments.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Extending a well-formed substitution pair]
\label{thm:expa-adequate-cons}

\begin{definition}[Weakening of the logical relation]
\label{def:expa-lristype-weak}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRIsType.weak',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LREqTy.weak',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRDefEq.weak',Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRIsType.cast,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LREqTy.cast,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRDefEq.cast,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRHasTy.cast}

\texttt{LRIsType.rec} in which the $\Pi$ case composes the two Kripke liftings and rewrites along

\begin{lemma}[Symmetry of semantic type equality, unproved]
\label{thm:expa-lreqty-symm-hole}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LREqTy.defeq_r,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LREqTy.symm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRDefEq.symm,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LREqTy.defeq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRDefEq.defeq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRDefEq.left,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LRIsType.hasType}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{243}
\uses{thm:expa-lristype-irrel}
Two claims. \texttt{LREqTy.defeq\_r}: a term equality held at a witness for $A$ can be transported
to a witness for any $B$ semantically equal to $A$. \texttt{LREqTy.symm}: from a witness for $A$
together with a semantic equality to $B$, produce a witness for $B$ that is semantically equal back
to $A$. The surrounding projections (\texttt{defeq}, \texttt{left}, \texttt{hasType}) and the
symmetry of term equality at a fixed witness are proved.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\uses{def:expa-logrel-classifier-forallE}
\textbf{Not proved in Lean.} \texttt{LREqTy.defeq\_r} is a bare \texttt{sorry}, and all three
branches of \texttt{LREqTy.symm} (\texttt{stuck}, \texttt{sort}, \texttt{forallE}) are
\texttt{sorry} with commented-out attempts beside them. Symmetry of the type-equality relation is
the standard hard obligation in logrel-mltt developments; it is where this branch stalled.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The semantic context and substitution layer]
\label{def:expa-logrelv}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.ClassifierV',Lean4Lean.SExpr.ClassifierV,Lean4Lean.SExpr.ClassifierV.IsTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LogRelV,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtx,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqSubst,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtx.nil,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtx.cons,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtx.inv,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtxInv,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsCtx.cons_inv,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqSubst.left,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqSubst.tail,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqSubst.head,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVIsType.subst,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqSubst.eqTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVEqTy,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVHasType,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVDefEq,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVDefEq.refl,Lean4Lean.SExpr.LVDefEq.symm}

\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{258}

symmetry.

\texttt{LVHasType.lift} survive only as commented-out stubs at lines 323--343.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Fundamental theorem, abandoned]
\label{thm:expa-logrel-fundamental}
\lean{Lean4Lean.SExpr.fundamental}
\leanok
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{353}
\uses{def:expa-logrelv}
The claim is that $\Gamma \vdash a \equiv b : A$ implies that, for every validated context, there is
a level $u$ and a semantically valid type at $u$ for $A$ at which $a$ and $b$ are semantically
equal. This is the predecessor of \ref{thm:expa-lr-adequacy}.
\thesisref{\texttt{unique.tex} Theorem \texttt{thm:1dinv} and \texttt{soundness.tex} Theorem
\texttt{thm:sound} in spirit; nothing here is actually established.}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\uses{thm:expa-lreqty-symm-hole}
\textbf{Not proved in Lean.} The 27-line induction covers only \texttt{bvar} (both sub-cases behind
\texttt{stop}), \texttt{symm} (the one rule actually discharged), \texttt{trans} (which ends in
\texttt{sorry} at line 375) and \texttt{sort} (behind \texttt{stop}); every remaining rule is
swallowed by a \texttt{| \_ => sorry} catch-all at line 379. Two literal \texttt{sorry}s and four
\texttt{stop}s leave the statement entirely unproved.
\end{proof}

\section{Review notes}

All four modules are master code; none of the issues below is attributable to the \texttt{iota} or
\texttt{trproj} branches.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{High.} \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRelAdequacy.lean}{154}: a live
\texttt{sorry} in the \texttt{const} case of \texttt{LR.adequacy}
(\ref{thm:expa-lr-adequacy}). Because that is the fundamental theorem, the three headline results
\ref{thm:expa-forallE-inv}, \ref{thm:expa-sort-forallE-inv} and \ref{thm:expa-sort-inv}, and
everything \texttt{Lean4Lean/Experimental/UniqueTyping.lean} derives from them, are unproved. Bare
constants are not a corner case of a real Lean term. The hole predates commit \texttt{84f2b04},
titled ``Finished injectivity!'', which landed the injectivity theorems and
\texttt{UniqueTyping.lean} on top of it without closing it; no comment in the source warns that a
case is missing.
\item \textbf{High.} \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5055}:
\texttt{LE\_Interp.strongSound} (\ref{thm:expa-le-interp-strongsound}), and likewise
\texttt{LR.adequacy} at \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRelAdequacy.lean}{109}, begin with
\texttt{replace H := H.strong}, where \texttt{SExpr.IsDefEq.strong} is a \texttt{sorry} at
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/SExpr.lean}{679}. Everything from line 5054 of
\texttt{ShapeLogRel.lean} onwards is therefore conditional on an unproved strengthening of the
definitional-equality judgement, on top of twenty-nine further \texttt{sorry}-bearing lines in that
file (thirty in all: \texttt{IsDefEqStrong.defeq} 680, \texttt{Params.ctor\_ty} 688, inversion at
735--738, substitution lemmas at 761--798, 825 and 886, \texttt{extra} and reduction cases at
957--1008, and 1069--1324). No file-level comment records this. The boundary is worth stating
precisely because it is narrow: 23 of the 818 declarations of \texttt{ShapeLogRel.lean} are
tainted, all of them at line 5054 or later.
\item \textbf{High.} \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRel.lean}{5232}: the $\delta$ and
$\iota$ (\texttt{extra}) case of \texttt{LE\_Interp.strongSound}, and of \texttt{LR.adequacy}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/ShapeLogRelAdequacy.lean}{431}), is
discharged with \texttt{axiom Params.extra\_pat}
(\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/SExpr.lean}{614}), a genuine axiom and not a class field: the
corresponding field is commented out of \texttt{class Params} at
\srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/SExpr.lean}{42}. The axiom asserts that every environment defeq is
an instance of a registered pattern rule, which is the hard content of $\iota$-reduction. It is
invisible from these files.
\item \textbf{Medium.} \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{353}: \texttt{fundamental}
(\ref{thm:expa-logrel-fundamental}) is unproved, and \texttt{LREqTy.defeq\_r} (243),
\texttt{LREqTy.symm} (246, all three branches), \texttt{LVIsType.sort} (287) and
\texttt{LVIsType.lift} (316) are \texttt{sorry} or \texttt{stop}. Since \texttt{stop tacticSeq}
macro-expands to \texttt{repeat sorry} in Lean core (\texttt{Init/Tactics.lean}), the five
\texttt{stop}s in this file (320, 361, 364, 369, 377) are silent sorries that a \texttt{grep} for
\texttt{sorry} does not find. The module is imported by nothing and is superseded
by \texttt{ShapeLogRel}; it should be deleted or explicitly marked abandoned.
\item \textbf{Medium.} \srcloc{Lean4Lean/Experimental/LogRel.lean}{70}: namespace collisions.
\texttt{Lean4Lean.SExpr.LogRel} is declared three times in this directory with incompatible
definitions (here as an inductive, \texttt{ShapeLogRel.lean}:5271 as a structure,
\texttt{MoreStepIndexed.lean}:371 as another structure); likewise
\texttt{Lean4Lean.SExpr.Classifier'} (\texttt{LogRel.lean}:8, \texttt{StepIndexed.lean}:8) and
\texttt{Lean4Lean.SExpr.NormalType} (\texttt{LogRel.lean}:58, \texttt{StepIndexed.lean}:41). These
